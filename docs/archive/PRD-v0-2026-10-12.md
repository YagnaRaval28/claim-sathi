# ClaimSathi — Product Requirements Document

Oct 12, 2026 · @Yagna

## Overview

ClaimSathi is a web app that helps Indian policyholders overturn rejected or partly-paid health insurance claims. The user uploads a rejection letter, policy wording and hospital papers. The AI then explains why the claim was rejected, checks that reason against the policy and IRDAI rules, drafts a cited appeal, and tracks the case through every escalation level.

**Vision:** No Indian family loses a valid claim just because the appeal process was too confusing.

**Product type:** Full-stack web app (responsive, mobile-first) with a GenAI back end.

**Status:** Concept / pre-MVP.

## Problem statement

Health insurance claim disputes in India are rising fast. Most policyholders lack the knowledge, time, or confidence to appeal.

| Indicator | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health claim complaints on Bima Bharosa, FY 2023-24 | 47,658 | [Lok Sabha reply, 30 Mar 2026](https://taxguru.in/?p=1035064) |
| Health claim complaints on Bima Bharosa, FY 2024-25 | 64,365 | [Lok Sabha reply, 30 Mar 2026](https://taxguru.in/?p=1035064) |
| Health claim complaints on Bima Bharosa, FY 2025-26 (to Feb 2026) | 73,729 | [Lok Sabha reply, 30 Mar 2026](https://taxguru.in/?p=1035064) |
| Complaints filed with Insurance Ombudsman offices, FY 2024-25 (all lines) | 53,102 | [Parliament answer](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/3015516/1/AS20_AaaDVE.pdf) |

**Why people lose valid claims:**

- **Rejections are hard to understand.** Letters cite clauses like "pre-existing disease" or "waiting period" with little explanation.
- **Many rejections come from fixable errors.** Name or age mismatches, missing reports, and hospital data-entry mistakes are common ([PayBima](https://www.paybima.com/blog/health-and-wellness/health-claim-denied-the-real-fault-may-be-a-documentation-error)).
- **Escalation has three levels and strict deadlines.** The route runs from the insurer's grievance officer to Bima Bharosa to the Insurance Ombudsman, and the Ombudsman must be approached within one year of rejection ([guide](https://righttoinformation.wiki/health-insurance-claim-rejection-complaint-2026)).
- **There is no automatic escalation.** Bima Bharosa does not forward unresolved complaints to the Ombudsman ([Parliament answer](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/3015516/1/AS20_AaaDVE.pdf)).

**Market gap:** In the US, AI appeal tools such as [Counterforce Health](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce), Claimable and Fight Health Insurance already turn a denial letter plus policy into a cited appeal. In India, current options are human-run claim-recovery agencies that charge a service fee. We found no self-serve AI tool built for IRDAI rules and Indian policy wordings. This should be re-checked before launch.

## Goals, non-goals and success metrics

**Goals**

1. Explain any health claim rejection in plain language, in under 2 minutes after upload.
2. Produce an appeal letter in which every argument cites a real policy clause or regulation.
3. Guide users through all three escalation levels without missing a deadline.
4. Support English, Hindi and Gujarati.

**Non-goals (for now)**

- Giving legal advice or representing users before the Ombudsman or a consumer court.
- Motor, life or travel insurance claims.
- Filing on insurer portals automatically on the user's behalf.
- Selling or recommending insurance policies.

**Success metrics.** Targets are proposed starting points to validate during the beta.

| Metric | Definition | Target (6 months after launch) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activation | Users who upload a rejection letter and get a diagnosis | 60% of sign-ups |
| Letter completion | Diagnosed cases that download or send an appeal | 50% |
| Citation accuracy | AI citations verified correct in manual audit | 98% or higher |
| Reported overturn rate | Cases where the user reports full or partial payout | Track baseline; target 40% |
| Time to first letter | Upload to downloadable draft | Under 5 minutes |
| User trust | Post-case rating of 4 or 5 out of 5 | 80% |

The primary user is a policyholder or family member whose claim was rejected or partly paid. Hospital billing desks are a secondary, paying segment for later.

## User journey

A user goes from rejection letter to a ready-to-send appeal in one session, then returns only when a deadline or reply needs action.

1. **Sign up** with phone OTP or Google, choose a language, and accept data consent.
2. **Create a case** and upload the rejection letter, policy schedule and wording, discharge summary, bills, and any insurer emails (PDF or photo).
3. **Review extracted details.** The app shows policy number, insurer, claim amount, dates and the rejection reason, and the user corrects anything wrong.
4. **Read the diagnosis.** A plain-language explanation of the rejection, the clause the insurer relied on, counter-points with sources, and a case-strength rating (Strong / Moderate / Weak) with reasons.
5. **Fill the gaps.** A checklist asks for missing evidence, such as a doctor's certificate stating when the condition was first diagnosed.
6. **Generate the appeal.** The user picks a tone and language, edits the draft in a rich-text editor, and downloads it as PDF or copies it into email.
7. **Send and log.** The user sends it to the insurer's grievance officer and records the send date.
8. **Track and escalate.** The app counts down the response window. If the insurer does not respond or rejects again, it prepares the Bima Bharosa complaint and then the Ombudsman complaint, carrying over all case details.
9. **Close the case.** The user records the outcome (paid, partly paid, rejected), which improves future case-strength scoring.

## AI / GenAI system design

The AI is a retrieval-grounded pipeline, not a free-form chatbot. Every statement in a diagnosis or letter must trace back to a stored source passage.

&#91;embedded content: AI pipeline · upload to user review, with a citation check\]

Documents flow left to right, then back along the second row. A draft only reaches the user after a check confirms that each citation exists in a retrieved source.

| Component | Approach | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OCR and clean-up | OpenCV pre-processing, then Tesseract (English, Hindi, Gujarati) for the MVP; a cloud document-AI service if accuracy is too low | Keep page and line positions so the UI can highlight source text |
| Field extraction | LLM with a strict JSON schema, validated with Pydantic | Low-confidence fields shown for user confirmation |
| Rejection classifier | Few-shot LLM prompt over the 8-type taxonomy | Fine-tune a small model once about 1,000 labelled cases exist |
| Knowledge base | Policy wordings chunked by clause; IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (29 May 2024); published Ombudsman awards | Metadata per chunk: insurer, product, version, effective date, clause number |
| Retrieval | Hybrid keyword (BM25) plus embedding search in pgvector, filtered by insurer and product, then re-ranked | Return top 8 chunks with IDs |
| Analysis and score | LLM reasons only over retrieved chunks; case strength starts as a transparent rule-based score plus similar-award outcomes | Show the reasons behind every score |
| Appeal drafting | Fixed letter skeleton (parties, facts, grounds, relief sought) filled by the LLM | Each paragraph carries its citation IDs |
| Citation verifier | Deterministic code checks that each quoted span appears in its cited chunk | Unsupported sentences are removed or flagged, never shown as fact |
| Escalation agent | State machine for stages; deadlines from a rules table, not the LLM; LLM drafts the next-level complaint | Reuses verified facts from earlier stages |
| Translation | LLM translation with a fixed glossary of insurance terms | Hindi and Gujarati templates reviewed by native speakers |

**Guardrails**

- Treat all text from uploaded documents as data, never as instructions, to block prompt injection.
- The model may not invent amounts, dates or clause numbers. These come only from extracted fields or retrieved chunks.
- Requests outside health insurance claims get a polite out-of-scope message.
- Keep a gold test set of 100 anonymised rejection letters, and run it on every prompt or model change, tracking extraction accuracy, classification accuracy and citation accuracy.

## Technical architecture

A Next.js front end talks to a FastAPI back end. Uploads and AI processing go to a job queue, so the web app stays fast and no upload is lost if an AI provider is slow.

&#91;embedded content: System architecture · clients, API, data stores, workers, external services\]

The API only creates cases and queues jobs. Workers do the slow OCR and LLM steps, write results to PostgreSQL, and the web app polls or receives updates over Server-Sent Events.

**Tech stack**

| Layer | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front end | Next.js (React, TypeScript), Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui, TipTap editor | Fast, SEO-friendly landing pages; good rich-text editing for letters |
| Back end | Python, FastAPI, Pydantic | Python has the best AI and OCR libraries |
| Jobs | Celery with Redis | Retries and rate limits for LLM calls |
| Database | PostgreSQL with pgvector | One store for app data and embeddings |
| Files | S3-compatible storage in an India region | Signed URLs, encryption at rest |
| AI | Hosted LLM API, an embedding model, Tesseract or a cloud OCR service | Swappable behind a provider interface |
| Auth | Phone OTP plus Google sign-in (e.g. Supabase Auth or Firebase Auth) | Phone-first users in India |
| PDF / DOCX export | WeasyPrint and python-docx | Server-side, consistent output |
| Hosting | Vercel (front end); Docker on AWS Mumbai or GCP Mumbai (back end) | India data residency |
| Monitoring | Sentry, an LLM tracing tool (e.g. Langfuse), uptime checks | Debug prompts and track costs |

## Non-functional requirements

Medical records are among the most sensitive data a user can share. Privacy and accuracy matter more than speed or extra features.

| Area | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Privacy and consent | Explicit, purpose-specific consent at upload, aligned with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; a plain-language privacy notice in all supported languages |
| Data minimisation | Remove or mask personal identifiers (name, address, phone) before text is sent to third-party LLM APIs where possible; never use user documents to train models without separate opt-in |
| Retention | Auto-delete source files 90 days after a case is closed unless the user extends it; one-click full deletion |
| Security | TLS in transit; AES-256 at rest; signed, short-lived URLs for files; role-based admin access with audit logs; OWASP Top 10 checks before launch |
| Data residency | Host storage and database in an India region |
| Accuracy | No AI claim without a retrievable source; low-confidence outputs flagged for user review; monthly citation audit on a 100-case sample |
| Performance | Diagnosis within 60 seconds for a 20-page upload (p90); letter generation under 30 seconds; UI interactions under 200 ms |
| Availability | 99.5% monthly uptime; queued processing so uploads are never lost during an LLM outage |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.1 AA; works on low-end Android phones over a 3G connection; large-text mode |
| Legal framing | Clear statement on every output that ClaimSathi is a self-help drafting tool, not legal advice |

## Risks and open questions

The biggest risk is a confident but wrong AI citation, because it can weaken a user's case. Grounding and verification are therefore launch blockers.

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI cites a clause or rule that does not exist | High: damages the user's case and trust | Retrieval-only citations, a post-generation verifier that string-matches each quote, and a human-reviewed sample |
| Seen as practising law without a licence | High: legal exposure | Position as a self-help drafting tool; the user always sends the letter; disclaimers; legal review of copy before launch |
| Leak of medical data | High: legal and reputational harm | Encryption, PII masking, India hosting, minimal retention, security audit |
| Policy wordings hard to collect | Medium: weaker analysis | Start with the top 10 retail health products; let users upload their own wording; crowd-source with consent |
| Poor OCR on phone photos | Medium: wrong extracted fields | Image clean-up before OCR, user confirmation of every field, re-upload prompts |
| Insurers change rules or wordings | Medium: outdated advice | Version the knowledge base with effective dates; quarterly refresh |
| Low willingness to pay | Medium: no business model | Keep consumers free; monetise clinics and consultants (v3) |

**Open questions**

- [ ] Are Ombudsman awards available in bulk and in a usable format, and what are the reuse terms?
- [ ] Which LLM provider offers suitable data-processing terms and an India region?
- [ ] Should the MVP support cashless deductions as well as full rejections, or full rejections only?
- [ ] Is a lawyer partnership needed for cases above a certain claim value?
